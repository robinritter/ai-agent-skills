# Worked scenarios

These are synthetic examples, not real holdings or live market assessments.

## Portfolio: transparent assumptions

Input: unlevered spot values of BTC $5,000, an alt group $3,000 and USDC $2,000.
The user supplies hypothetical BTC betas of 1, 1.5 and 0, with no depeg.

My first concern is concentration: BTC is 50% of the observed $10,000 portfolio,
and the top three positions are 100%. Both screening thresholds are triggered.
I would stress 80% of your observed capital as sharing crypto-market exposure;
that does not establish that 80% of statistical risk comes from Bitcoin.

| Group | Value | Weight | Assumed beta | Loss at BTC −30% | Loss at BTC −50% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BTC | $5,000 | 50% | 1 | $1,500 | $2,500 |
| Alt group | $3,000 | 30% | 1.5 | $1,350 | $2,250 |
| USDC | $2,000 | 20% | 0, no depeg | $0 | $0 |
| Total | $10,000 | 100% | — | $2,850 / 28.5% | $4,750 / 47.5% |

Post-shock values are $7,150 and $5,250. These are conditional linear stress
scenarios, not predicted losses. Betas and correlations can change. USDC retains
peg/custody risk even though its assumed BTC beta is zero. I would first evaluate
whether less high-beta exposure fits your drawdown limit; that also sacrifices
some upside. I cannot set a suitable allocation without your risk context.

## News: familiar is not proof of pricing

Input: “ETF inflows hit a record,” repeated for three weeks; no underlying release,
expectations or price data. Browsing is unavailable.

My classification is **previously known; pricing not established**. I cannot tell
from repetition whether the latest number differs from expectations. I would check
the dated primary release and the incremental surprise before treating it as a
reason to trade. Nothing supplied establishes an actionable edge.

## Trade: challenge the action actually proposed

Input: “I plan to sell after my long-term fundamental thesis failed. Position 5%,
no leverage.”

Your strongest case is that the reason you owned the asset no longer holds, and
you want capital allocated to a thesis you can defend. The opposing case is that
you could be mistaking a temporary setback for permanent failure and exiting
before recovery. I would test the specific observation that invalidated the thesis,
what evidence would reverse it, and what the replacement use of capital offers.
No bias is established by those words. My verdict is **insufficient detail to
judge**, with evidence of a thesis-based approach. The condition I would write
down is the observable fundamental failure that makes the exit justified; a fixed
price stop is not the only way to make a long-term decision testable.

## Fundamental analysis: missing evidence

Input: a verified project identity and price, but no usable security, supply,
token-capture or valuation evidence.

I would mark those dimensions **U** and the overall score **not rated — incomplete
evidence**. My verdict is **Wait**, with the specific missing checks. I cannot
turn an unavailable unlock schedule into zero releases or marketing about an audit
into verified security. A full report keeps all 18 headings and states the gaps.
